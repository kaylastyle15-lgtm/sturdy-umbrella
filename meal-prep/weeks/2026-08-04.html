Sausage & Chicken Rigatoni in Rustic Sugo

Italian · serves 2 · ~40 min active

You'll use

  • 8 oz chicken thighs, diced
  • Italian chicken sausage, casings removed
  • 28 oz crushed tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 3 garlic cloves, diced
  • 1 tsp fennel seed, chili flake, dried oregano
  • 6 oz rigatoni (half the box)
  • Basil, Pecorino or lactose-free ricotta

Method

  1. Brown the sausage and chicken hard in olive oil, breaking up the sausage — don't crowd the pan, work in two batches if needed. Remove.
  2. In the same pan, soften onion and garlic in the rendered fat. Toast the fennel seed and chili flake for 30 seconds until fragrant — this is where the flavor actually comes from, don't skip it.
  3. Add crushed tomatoes, scrape up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan, return the meat, simmer uncovered 20–25 min until it thickens.
  4. Boil rigatoni to just under al dente, toss into the sugo with a splash of pasta water to bring it together.
  5. Finish with torn basil and shaved Pecorino (or a dollop of lactose-free ricotta).
Meal-prep honesty: pasta is the weak link here. It holds fine for 2–3 days but gets soft and drinks up the sauce past that — that's why it's slotted Mon and Fri, not late in the week. If you want to push it further out, cook the pasta noticeably under al dente and store sauce and noodles separately, combining only when reheating.

Braised Beef Barbacoa with Cilantro-Lime Rice

Mexican · serves 2 · ~15 min active, ~2.5 hr mostly-unattended

You'll use

  • 1 lb beef chuck, cut into large chunks
  • 2 chipotles in adobo + 1 tbsp sauce
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp oregano, 3 garlic cloves, half an onion
  • Juice of 1 lime, ½ cup broth, 1 bay leaf
  • ¾ cup rice, cilantro, lime zest
  • Frozen corn, radish, jalapeño for salsa

Method

  1. Sear the chuck hard on all sides in a dutch oven — real color, not just grey. This is most of your flavor base.
  2. Blend or mash the chipotles, adobo sauce, cumin, oregano, garlic, onion, and broth into a rough paste; pour over the beef with the bay leaf.
  3. Cover, braise low (300°F oven or low simmer on the stove) 2–2.5 hours until it shreds with a fork.
  4. Shred, toss back through the braising liquid so it stays juicy, finish with lime juice.
  5. Char the corn dry in a hot skillet, toss with diced radish, jalapeño, lime, salt for a crunchy salsa. Serve over cilantro-lime rice.
Meal-prep honesty: this is the best keeper of the four. Braised beef improves after a day or two in the fridge and freezes well. Keep the corn salsa separate and add fresh at serving — it goes soggy and loses its crunch if it sits mixed in.

Chicken & Chickpea Tagine-Style Stew — new cuisine

Moroccan · serves 2 · ~45 min

You'll use

  • 12 oz chicken thighs, chunked
  • 15 oz can chickpeas, drained
  • ⅓ cup dried apricots, chopped
  • 1 onion, 3 garlic cloves, 1" fresh ginger
  • 2 tsp ras el hanout, 1 tbsp tomato paste, 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 cup broth, ⅔ cup couscous, toasted almonds, parsley

Method

  1. Sear the chicken chunks lightly, remove. Soften onion, garlic, ginger in the same pot.
  2. Add the tomato paste and ras el hanout directly to the fat and stir for 60–90 seconds until it darkens and smells toasted — this step is what separates a real tagine flavor from "chicken with curry powder dumped in."
  3. Deglaze with broth, add the cinnamon stick, chickpeas, apricots, and chicken back in.
  4. Simmer uncovered 20–25 min until the sauce clings and the apricots have softened into it.
  5. Serve over couscous (just pour boiling water or broth over it, cover, 5 min, fluff), topped with toasted almonds and parsley.
New cuisine — Moroccan: the defining move is building a warm-spice base (cinnamon, ginger, cumin, coriander, turmeric — usually pre-blended as ras el hanout) and balancing it against something sweet, here dried fruit, so the dish reads savory-forward with a background sweetness rather than tasting like dessert spices in a stew.
Meal-prep honesty: holds and freezes well, actually improves by day 2 as the apricots keep releasing sweetness into the sauce. Couscous is best made fresh each night (5 minutes, no real prep-ahead benefit) rather than batched.

Dry-Brined Blackened Chicken with Smoky Red Beans & Rice — new technique

Cajun · serves 2 · brine ahead + ~25 min active

You'll use

  • 12 oz chicken thighs
  • Kosher salt for the dry brine
  • Paprika, cayenne, garlic powder, onion powder, thyme, oregano, black pepper
  • 15 oz can kidney beans
  • Half onion, half bell pepper, 1 celery stalk, 2 garlic cloves — the "holy trinity"
  • Smoked paprika, bay leaf, hot sauce, ¾ cup rice, green onion

Method

  1. Salt the chicken thighs directly (about ¾ tsp kosher salt per pound) and leave uncovered in the fridge 4–24 hours ahead. Pat bone-dry before cooking — do not rinse.
  2. Coat with the blackening spice mix. Sear in a screaming-hot cast iron pan, 3–4 min a side, until deeply charred.
  3. Separately, sweat the onion, pepper, celery, garlic in a splash of oil until soft, add smoked paprika and bay leaf, then the beans and enough water to loosen slightly. Simmer 15 min, mash a few beans against the pot to thicken.
  4. Serve the chicken sliced over rice and beans, hot sauce and green onion on top.
New technique — dry-brining: salting meat well ahead (not right before cooking) gives the salt time to dissolve, pull moisture out, then get reabsorbed back in along with it — seasoning goes all the way through instead of just sitting on the surface, and the meat holds onto more moisture when it hits high heat. No rinsing after, unlike a wet brine — just pat it dry so it sears instead of steams.
Meal-prep honesty: the beans and rice reheat great. The chicken is the risk — that hard sear is easy to overcook on reheat and turn dry. Reheat it gently, sliced, with a splash of broth or covered in the microwave at 70% power rather than full blast, or undercook it very slightly the first time.
