Beef Goulash over Egg Noodles — new cuisine

Hungarian · serves 2 · ~15 min active, ~2–2.5 hr mostly-unattended
Source: adapted from RecipeTin Eats' Hungarian Goulash — Nagi Maehashi's recipes are developed through repeated home testing and are consistently among the most reliable, reader-vetted versions of classic dishes online. Adapted here: scaled down to a 2-serving batch from her larger yield, caraway made optional since it's a less common pantry item, egg noodles used as the starch instead of her suggested bread/mash for a heartier one-bowl dinner.

You'll use

  • 1 lb beef chuck, cut into 1.5" chunks
  • 2 tbsp sweet Hungarian paprika (not your regular paprika)
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 red bell pepper
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste, 2 cups beef broth, 1 bay leaf
  • 6 oz egg noodles
  • Sour cream (or lactose-free alt) and parsley to finish

Method

  1. Sear the beef hard in batches — don't crowd the pot, or it steams instead of browning. Remove.
  2. Soften the onion and bell pepper in the same pot. Pull the pot off the heat, stir in the paprika, and let it sit 20–30 seconds before adding liquid — paprika scorches and turns bitter over direct high heat, so this off-heat blooming step matters.
  3. Add garlic, tomato paste, bay leaf, broth, and the beef back in. Cover and simmer low 2–2.5 hours until fork-tender.
  4. Boil the egg noodles just before serving each night — they're quick enough not to need pre-cooking.
  5. Serve the goulash over noodles with a dollop of sour cream (or lactose-free alt) and chopped parsley.
Meal-prep honesty: the stew itself is a great keeper — improves overnight, freezes well, same category as barbacoa was last week. Cook the noodles fresh each night rather than batching them; they only take 8–10 minutes and pre-cooked noodles left sitting in the fridge for days turn gummy.

Lemon Chicken & Orzo — new technique

Greek · serves 2 · ~35 min
Source: adapted from The Mediterranean Dish's One-Pan Greek Chicken and Orzo — Suzy Karadsheh's site is built specifically around tested, reader-reviewed Mediterranean cooking. Adapted here: stovetop instead of her baked version so it fits a 35-minute cook-session slot, feta made optional given your lactose sensitivity, quantities scaled to 2 servings.

You'll use

  • 12 oz chicken thighs
  • 1 lemon (juice + zest), 3 garlic cloves, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 cup orzo (dry), 2 cups chicken broth
  • Olive oil
  • Feta (optional) or lactose-free alt, fresh dill or parsley

Method

  1. Season the chicken and sear hard in olive oil until deeply golden on both sides — it won't be cooked through yet. Remove.
  2. Off the heat, pour in a splash of the broth and the lemon juice, then scrape the bottom of the pan with a wooden spoon — those browned bits (the fond) are concentrated flavor, not something to wash away.
  3. Add the rest of the broth, garlic, and oregano, bring to a simmer, stir in the dry orzo, and nestle the chicken back on top.
  4. Cover and cook 12–15 minutes until the orzo is tender and most of the liquid is absorbed.
  5. Finish with lemon zest, chopped dill or parsley, and crumbled feta if using.
New technique — deglazing / building a pan sauce from the fond: after searing meat, the browned bits stuck to the pan are flavor, not mess. A splash of liquid — broth, wine, citrus juice, even water — lifts them back into the dish with a wooden spoon, and that becomes the base of your sauce instead of an empty pan you're about to scrub. Same move works in French, Italian, and here, Greek cooking.
Meal-prep honesty: orzo firms up and can turn a little gluey past 2–3 days — this dish only needs to hold from Tuesday's cook to Wednesday's dinner, so it's fine as-is. If you ever stretch it further, undercook the orzo slightly and loosen with a splash of broth on reheat.

Thai Basil Chicken (Pad Krapow Gai)

Thai · serves 2 · ~20 min
Source: adapted from Hot Thai Kitchen's Pad Kra Pao — Pailin Chongchitnant is a professionally trained Thai cooking instructor whose recipes are widely regarded as the standard for accessible, authentic home versions of Thai dishes. Adapted here: ground chicken instead of her typical ground pork option to fit your protein preferences, quantities scaled to 2 servings.

You'll use

  • 14 oz ground chicken
  • 4–5 garlic cloves, 2–3 Thai or serrano chilies (or chili flakes), minced/pounded together
  • 1.5 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tsp soy sauce, pinch of sugar
  • 1.5 cups fresh basil leaves
  • Jasmine rice, 2 eggs

Method

  1. Cook the rice.
  2. Pound or mince the garlic and chilies into a rough paste.
  3. Stir-fry the paste in hot oil ~20 seconds until fragrant, add the ground chicken, and break it up as it cooks through.
  4. Add oyster sauce, fish sauce, soy sauce, and sugar, toss to coat.
  5. Off the heat, fold in the basil just until wilted — it turns dull and loses aroma if it cooks too long.
  6. Fry an egg per serving with crispy edges and a runny yolk. Serve the chicken over rice, egg on top.
Meal-prep honesty, twofold: first, authentic pad krapow uses Thai holy basil, which none of your four stores reliably carry — regular Italian basil (on the shopping list) is the standard substitute and gets you close, but the flavor is genuinely a step removed from the real thing. Second, the fried egg and basil don't hold — cook those fresh each night, not ahead. The stir-fried chicken itself reheats fine in a hot pan; just add a fresh egg and freshly torn basil at serving both nights.

Weeknight Beef Ragù, Short Pasta

Italian · serves 2 · ~30 min
Source: adapted from Bon Appétit's Weeknight Ragù (Chris Morocco) — a widely-cooked, well-reviewed recipe built specifically to compress a slow-cooked-tasting ragù into a weeknight timeline. Adapted here: lean ground beef instead of the original's pork/turkey option to fit your protein preferences, cream made optional/lactose-free given your sensitivity, scaled to 2 servings.

You'll use

  • 1 lb lean ground beef (90/10)
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, red pepper flakes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 6 oz short pasta (gemelli or similar)
  • Splash of cream or lactose-free cream, grated cheese to finish (optional)

Method

  1. Brown the beef hard in a wide pan, breaking it into small pieces — don't drain the fat, it's flavor.
  2. Add the onion, garlic, and red pepper flakes, cook until the onion softens.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1–2 minutes until it darkens and smells toasted, same principle as last week's tagine — don't skip this, it's most of the depth in a quick sauce like this.
  4. Add a splash of water or broth, simmer 15–20 minutes until it thickens into a real sauce.
  5. Boil the pasta, toss with the ragù and a splash of pasta water, finish with a swirl of cream (or lactose-free alt) and grated cheese.
Meal-prep honesty: the ragù itself is a strong keeper, right up there with the goulash. If you use the dairy cream, add it fresh at serving rather than simmering it into the batch ahead of time — it can split slightly on reheat. The lactose-free alternatives behave the same way, so treat it as a finishing touch either way, not a batched ingredient.
